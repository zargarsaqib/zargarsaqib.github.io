About

I am a PhD candidate in Environmental Engineering at IIT Madras, advised by Dr. Chandan Sarangi, with a thesis defence expected in February 2027. My thesis examines light-absorbing aerosols in Western Himalayan seasonal snow: their concentrations, radiative forcing, and detection from hyperspectral satellite data.

As a Visiting Student Researcher at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory (2025–26), I developed a DISORT-based retrieval of dust in snow from EMIT imaging spectroscopy, grounded in Himalayan field spectroscopy and snow sampling. I also use in-situ measurements to constrain and evaluate SNICAR and WRF-Chem simulations of snow darkening. My fieldwork has included Himalayan campaigns from 2022 to 2026.

Research

Ablation-season snowfall, aerosol wet deposition and snow darkening in High Mountain Asia

2022 – present IIT Madras
  • Showed from satellite observations (2000–2020) that ablation-season (March–June) snow-cover duration and low-intensity snowfall frequency increased across the Western Himalaya and Hindu Kush, most strongly at mid-elevations. I attributed this trend to a deepening upper-tropospheric trough and moisture convergence.
  • Linked the snowfall increase to the seasonal peak in aerosol loading: greater LAP wet deposition drives rising satellite-derived snow darkening and radiative forcing. In this setting, more snowfall can accelerate glacier melt rather than buffer it.

Speciated light-absorbing particles and radiative forcing in Western Himalayan snow

Dec 2021 – Dec 2024 IIT Madras · DRDO-funded project
  • Built a depth- and time-resolved record of black carbon, brown carbon and dust in surface snow through the pre-monsoon melt season, with concurrent aerosol, deposition-flux and meteorological observations. Loading was dust-dominated and governed by episodic westerly transport and precipitation timing.
  • Quantified LAP radiative forcing with SNICAR, validated against spectroradiometer retrievals. Brown carbon contributes a non-negligible share of absorption and photobleaches as the snowpack ages, so models should neither omit it nor treat it as spectrally static.

Remote retrieval of dust-on-snow from NASA EMIT imaging spectroscopy

Mar 2025 – Feb 2026 NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory, Pasadena (Visiting Student Researcher) / IIT Madras
  • Developed a DISORT radiative-transfer inversion that retrieves dust concentration in snow from EMIT hyperspectral snow reflectance and albedo products.
  • Applied the retrieval to Himalayan scenes (Sinthan Pass, Razdan Pass) and a growing set of High Mountain Asia scenes, and assessed the quality and uncertainties of EMIT snow products.

Regional modelling of LAP deposition on Himalayan snow with WRF-Chem

Apr 2023 – present IIT Madras
  • Configured WRF-Chem for LAP deposition on snow. Traced and fixed a CLM land-surface bug that caused spurious snow loss over Himalayan grid cells.
